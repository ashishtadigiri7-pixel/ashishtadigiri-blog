Key takeaways

  • Count every variation you tested. After 240 tries on 4 years of data, luck alone produces an expected best Sharpe of 1.41.
  • For 95% confidence that the edge is real, you need roughly (luck hurdle + 1.645) ÷ √years That works out at 2.24 in the example, well above the 1.60 the strategy shows.
  • Run the checks in order. A strategy that fails check 2 (costs) has nothing for checks 3 to 7 to measure.

01What CME's record quarter means for your backtest

CME Group reported its third-quarter 2026 results on 21 October. Average daily volume reached 29.4 million contracts, and non-US volume hit a Q3 high of 9 million. Equity index futures and options averaged 7.71 million contracts a day, up 23% from 6.28 million in the third quarter of 2025.

The busy days kept coming after the quarter closed. On 26 October, after Brazil's election, CME's Brazilian real futures traded 498,498 contracts, more than double the previous record of 191,325 set on 28 September, and about eleven times the year-to-date daily average in notional terms.

A backtest fitted on a calmer stretch has never seen this tape. That says nothing about whether your strategy works. It does mean you should run the checks below again before you trust the equity curve, because fills, spreads and the regime your rules depend on may have changed under it.

Pass marks for checks 2, 6 and 7 are working rules of thumb. Check 4 follows Bailey and López de Prado's deflated Sharpe ratio. The 50% bar in check 5 is the rule of thumb TradeStation's walk-forward optimiser states for walk-forward efficiency.

Check 1, the truncation test. Run the backtest on data that ends on, say, 30 June, then on the full history. Every signal up to 30 June must match. If one moves, an indicator is reading bars that did not exist yet: a repainting pivot, a daily high used before the close, or a fill at the same bar's close that generated the signal. Fix that before you look at any profit figure.

03Worked example: an MNQ backtest that looks good and fails check 4

A hypothetical intraday strategy on Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 futures. MNQ is $2 times the index with a 0.25-point tick, so one tick is worth $0.50. The researcher tried 240 parameter combinations on 4 years of in-sample data and kept the best one.

Costs in test: commission + fees, slippage$1.50 + 1 tick per side ($1.00)

Check 2, costs. Doubling slippage adds another $1.00 per round turn. Net per trade falls from $15.20 to $14.20 against $3.50 of total cost: 4.1 times cost. Pass.

Check 3, trades. t = 15.20 ÷ 118 × √610 = 3.18. Pass. You would need about (2 × 118 ÷ 15.20)² = 241 trades at this average and spread to reach t = 2.

Check 4, luck. With 240 variations, the expected best Sharpe from strategies with no edge at all is 1.41 over 4 years. The deflated Sharpe ratio for 1.60 comes out at 65%: about a two-in-three chance the edge is real. Fail. A t-stat of 3.18 looked convincing because it treated the winner as the only test run.

Check 5, unseen data. 0.70 ÷ 1.60 = 44%. Fail, which fits what check 4 found: picking the best of 240 produced much of that 1.60.

04Backtest overfitting: how many variations can you afford?

The luck hurdle comes from the expected maximum of N draws from a normal distribution, scaled to your sample. Bailey and López de Prado give the expected maximum as (1 − γ) × Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γ × Φ⁻¹(1 − 1/(N × e)), where γ is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, 0.5772. With no real skill, the standard error of a Sharpe estimate is about 1/√T (Lo, 2002), which turns the hurdle into a simple annual figure:

luck hurdle = E[max of N] ÷ √years
Sharpe needed (95%) ≈ (E[max of N] + 1.645) ÷ √years

For 240 variations, E[max] = 2.825. On 4 years, the hurdle is 2.825 ÷ 2 = 1.41 and the Sharpe you need is (2.825 + 1.645) ÷ 2 = 2.24. Matching the hurdle gets you a coin flip; the extra 1.645 standard errors buy the 95%.

Annual Sharpe needed for a deflated Sharpe ratio of about 95%, assuming independent variations and normal returns. Correlated variations count as fewer independent trials, so the table overstates the bar for a grid of near-identical settings and understates it if returns have fat left tails.

Read the table in both directions. If you have 5 years of data and your best result is a 1.5 Sharpe, you can afford about 10 honest variations. If you have already run 1,000, a 2.0 on 5 years is not enough. The cheapest fix can be more history: going from 4 to 10 years on 240 variations drops the bar from 2.23 to 1.41.

05Checks 6 and 7: parameter plateaus and regime splits

Check 6. Lay your two most important parameters out as a grid and look at the cells around the one you picked. A real effect fades step by step as you move away from the best setting, while a fitted one collapses one cell out.

Check 7. Split the in-sample trades into two halves by volatility, for example days above and below the median 20-day ATR, and check that each half nets a profit. Then delete the best 1% of trades (6 of 610 in the example) and re-run the totals. If the profit sits in one regime or a handful of outlier days, the strategy is a bet that those conditions return, and a live account will meet a different regime within a quarter or two. A record-volume quarter like Q3 2026 is the moment to run this split again, with the new months included.
