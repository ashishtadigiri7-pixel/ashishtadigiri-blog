This week Bulenox lowered the drawdown on its Momentum accounts, and Lucid moved its LucidFlex consistency limit from 50% to 40%. Neither change touches your strategy. Both change how many contracts you can carry and how big your best day is allowed to be. On a Bulenox 100K, last week's size now runs on a quarter less room.

What to do this week

  1. Recalculate size from the distance to your floor: room ÷ (stop × point value × losses to survive).
  2. On LucidFlex, set a daily stop-win at 40% of your profit target ($1,200 on a 50K).
  3. Check your best-day share before paying for the 50% add-on. Under 40%, it buys you nothing.

02Size from the floor, not the balance

Room is the distance between your balance and the level where the account fails. On day one it equals the full drawdown. Losses to survive is how many full stop-outs in a row the account must absorb before it fails.

max contracts = room ÷ (stop in points × point value × losses to survive)

Worked example: MNQ at $2 per point, a 20-point stop, and enough room to survive 5 losses in a row. Each contract needs 20 × $2 × 5 = $200 of room.

Bulenox still lets a 100K trade 120 micros. The new drawdown supports 15 at this stop. The contract cap was never the limit that mattered.

Re-run the number every time the room changes. Halfway to the floor on the new 100K ($1,500 of room), the same setup supports 7 contracts, not 15. Most accounts that fail near breakeven are still trading day-one size.

03The 40% rule sets your daily stop-win

Lucid's consistency rule caps your best day as a share of total profit. At 40%, one big day raises the amount you need before you can pass. Turn the rule around and it gives you the number you need:

profit needed = best day ÷ 0.40
daily stop-win = 0.40 × profit target

Worked example on a LucidFlex 50K, which has a $3,000 target:

  • Best day $1,200: you need $1,200 ÷ 0.40 = $3,000. Exactly the target.
  • Best day $1,800: you need $4,500 at 40%, against $3,600 under the old 50% rule. One good day adds $1,500 of profit, and the risk that comes with it, before you qualify.

So the rule becomes a daily stop-win: $1,200 on the 50K, $2,400 on the 100K ($6,000 target), $3,600 on the 150K ($9,000 target). Once you are up that much on the day, stop. Profit above it doesn't help you pass. It raises the bar.

The same arithmetic explains Lucid's marketing line. At 40%, no single day can exceed 40% of the total, so you need at least 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.5, rounded up to 3 winning days. At 50% it is 2. That is the "as few as 3 days" on their page.

04Should you pay for the 50% add-on?

Pull your last 20 trading days and work out your best-day share: your largest single day divided by net profit over the period. Then:

Best-day shareDecision
Under 40%Skip the add-on. It buys you nothing.
40% to 50%Price it against the extra days you would otherwise need to trade.
Over 50%Neither cap fits how you make money. A daily stop-win is the fix, not the add-on.

Lucid's evaluation page states the 40% and 50% levels but not the exact calculation method. Reports describe it as best day against total profit. Confirm in your own dashboard before relying on it.

05Why this belongs in code, not in your head

These numbers move often. One public tracker logged more than a dozen futures prop-firm rule changes between 15 and 23 October alone. A rule you have to remember mid-session is a rule you will break when the session gets busy.

The fix is a risk layer that reads the room, the stop and the firm's limits before every order. That is the calculation inside Project Vector, my automated NQ and ES system, which enforces daily loss limits and trailing drawdown on its own. It is also the job of Project Vault, the futures risk console I built for live position sizing on Nasdaq and gold futures. Size comes from the floor, every time, without a spreadsheet.
