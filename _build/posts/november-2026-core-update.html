Google started the November 2026 core update on 10 November. Its status dashboard says the rollout "may take up to 2 weeks to complete", which runs to 24 November, three days before Black Friday. Google's advice is to wait a full week after it finishes, then compare a week of Search Console data with the week before the update. This year that "full week" lands on Black Friday and Cyber Monday, when traffic jumps for reasons that have nothing to do with the update. Compare the weeks Google's way and seasonal growth can hide a real loss. This note gives you a way to strip the season out with last year's numbers, a rule for what to do with the result, and a calculator that runs it.

Key takeaways

  • The rollout can run until 24 November. Until it ends, any change you see is unfinished, and any edit you make blurs the reading.
  • Divide this year's change by last year's change over the same weeks. That gives you the update's effect with the holiday ramp taken out.
  • Inside ±10% after adjustment, leave the site alone. Below −10%, run a full triage on the pages that lost ground.

01What is the November 2026 core update?

A core update is a broad change to how Google ranks pages, not a penalty and not aimed at any one kind of site. Google's Search Status Dashboard logged this one at 10:06 Pacific time on 10 November 2026, and says it applies worldwide and to all languages.

02Why a week-on-week comparison fails in November

Google's core update guidance says to confirm the rollout has finished, wait "at least a full week" after it completes, and compare that week "with a week before" the update started. That works in a quiet month. In late November, the week after the update is the busiest shopping week of the year for many sites, so traffic rises whether the update helped you or not.

The fix is to measure how much your traffic normally grows across the same two weeks, using last year's Search Console data, and take that growth out. Search Console keeps 16 months of performance data, so last November is still there.

03The seasonal adjustment: one division

Adjusted change = (this year after ÷ this year before) ÷ (last year after ÷ last year before) − 1

To make the weeks line up, go back 364 days, not one calendar year. That keeps Tuesday matched with Tuesday and Black Friday with Black Friday:

WindowThis yearLast year (364 days earlier)
Before the updateTue 3 to Mon 9 Nov 2026Tue 4 to Mon 10 Nov 2025
After the updateTue 1 to Mon 7 Dec 2026Tue 2 to Mon 8 Dec 2025

If the rollout finishes later than 24 November, move both "after" windows forward by the same number of days. In Search Console, open Performance, set the date range to Compare, enter each pair, and note total clicks. Do the same with impressions if you want to see whether visibility or click-through changed.

04Worked example: one shop, two outcomes

A hypothetical online shop, clicks from Search Console:

BeforeAfterRaw change
Last year (2025)7,50010,500+40%
This year, case A8,00010,400+30%
This year, case B8,0009,200+15%

Case A: 1.30 ÷ 1.40 − 1 = −7.1%. Traffic grew 30%, but the shop normally grows 40% across these weeks. A 7% shortfall is inside the noise band. Change nothing.

Case B: 1.15 ÷ 1.40 − 1 = −17.9%. The raw number still says +15%, and on Google's week-on-week view it looks like a good month. Against its own seasonal pattern, the shop lost nearly a fifth of its search traffic. That is a real drop, and it would have gone unnoticed until January.

Decision rule

  • Adjusted change between −10% and +10%: noise. Don't touch pages that rank.
  • Below −10%: run the Google ranking drop triage on the same two weeks to find which pages and queries lost ground, and whether visibility or click-through fell.
  • Above +10%: note which pages gained, and leave them alone. Don't let a winning page get rewritten for the sales.
  • No data from last year (new site): use Google's week-on-week check, and treat any gain in late November as seasonal until December data proves otherwise.

05What to do between now and Black Friday

  • Freeze content on pages that rank. Google advises against "quick fix" changes during core updates. An edit made mid-rollout also makes it impossible to tell whether the update or your change moved the page.
  • Do fix anything broken. Errors, pages dropping out of the index, slow checkout pages and broken product feeds have nothing to do with the update. Fix them the day you find them.
  • Write down your dates now. Note when the rollout ends on Google's status dashboard, then pull your two pairs of weeks one full week later.
  • Don't lean on search alone for the sale. If organic traffic carries your Black Friday, line up email and any paid campaigns now, so a mid-rollout dip doesn't cost you the week.
