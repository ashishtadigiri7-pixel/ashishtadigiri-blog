Four unconfirmed Google spikes in 12 days. Triage your ranking drop in 10 minutes.

Rank trackers spiked on 16, 20, 24 and 27 October and Google confirmed none of it. Split your click drop into impressions and CTR, run five Search Console checks in order, and know whether to fix your site or wait.

Rank trackers spiked four times in twelve days this October: on the 16th, on the 20th and 21st, on the 24th, and hardest on the 27th. As of 1 November, Google had confirmed none of it. If you saw a Google ranking drop in that window, an announcement may never come to tell you whether the cause is Google or your own site. Search Console already holds the answer. This note gives you a 10-minute triage, a formula that splits a click drop into its parts, and a tool that returns the next step.

Key takeaways

  • Google's last confirmed change is the September 2026 spam update, which ran from 24 September to 8 October. Its Search Status Dashboard shows no incident since.
  • Clicks = impressions × CTR. Work out which of the two fell before you touch a page: each points to a different fix.
  • Rule out manual actions, security issues and a single broken folder first. Treat a drop as algorithmic only once those checks come back clean.

01Was there a Google update in October 2026?

Not one Google has confirmed. The September 2026 spam update started on 24 September and finished on 8 October. On 1 November, Google's Search Status Dashboard still listed no ranking incident for the weeks since.

Search Engine Roundtable has logged four bursts of unconfirmed volatility across 15 tracking tools, including Semrush, Sistrix, Mozcast and AccuRanker:

  • 16 October: a spike in tools and forum chatter.
  • 20 and 21 October: a second wave. Glenn Gabe reported two sites in one niche that dropped out of results, even for their own brand names, and surged back on the evening of 16 October.
  • 24 October: a third wave, which Barry Schwartz said was "released Friday and into Saturday".
  • 27 October: the largest spike of the month, with Semrush at 9.4. Two days later Schwartz wrote that volatility "continues to get more heated by the day". Google still had not confirmed anything.

The same reports carry a warning about the tools themselves. Gabe found Ahrefs, Semrush and Sistrix disagreeing on when one drop happened, and said the trackers "are not picking up the entire picture". Your tracker's chart is the weakest evidence you have this month. Run the triage on Search Console, which reports Google's own data for your site.

02Search Console traffic drop: impressions or CTR?

Every Search Console click comes from an impression and a click-through rate. A drop in clicks therefore has two possible sources: Google showed you less (impressions), or people clicked less when it did (CTR). Logarithms split the change into two shares that add up to 100%:

impression share = ln(Iafter ÷ Ibefore) ÷ ln(Cafter ÷ Cbefore)
CTR share = 100% − impression share

Compare two windows of the same length that start on the same weekday. For a first read today, use 9 to 15 October (after the spam update, before the first spike) against 23 to 29 October. Both run Friday to Thursday. The second window holds the 24 and 27 October spikes, so run it again on 30 October to 5 November once that data lands.

Worked example (hypothetical site). Search Console shows 12,400 clicks and 410,000 impressions for the first window, then 9,300 clicks and 395,000 impressions for the second.

Clicks12,4009,300−25.0%
Impressions410,000395,000−3.7%

ln(395,000 ÷ 410,000) = −0.037. ln(9,300 ÷ 12,400) = −0.288. Impressions explain 0.037 ÷ 0.288 = 13% of the drop and CTR explains 87%. Google still shows this site almost as often and at almost the same position. Fewer people click, so the fix lies in the results page, and a content rewrite won't touch it.

Change two inputs and the answer flips. If clicks fall to 8,060 and impressions to 287,000, with position on top queries sliding from 7.9 to 12.6, impressions explain 83% of the drop. That site lost visibility, and the next question is whether Google or the site caused it.

03The 10-minute Google ranking drop triage

Run these in order and stop at the first check that finds something. The order matters: the early checks have one clear fix, while the last one means waiting.

  1. Manual actions and security issues (1 minute). Open both reports in Search Console. An entry in either explains the drop on its own, and Google tells you what to fix.
  2. Concentration (3 minutes). In Performance, filter Pages by your main folders (/blog/, /products/, /services/) and compare clicks across the two windows. If one folder holds 60% or more of the lost clicks, look at what changed there: a template deploy, a stray noindex, a canonical pointing elsewhere, redirects that broke. Google's own guide notes that a page-level problem such as a misplaced noindex causes a slower drop than a site-wide one.
  3. The split (2 minutes). Run the formula above. If CTR carries most of the drop, check your top 20 queries in a private window for new AI Overviews, video blocks or rich results above you, then rewrite titles and snippets for the queries that lost the most clicks.
  4. Position on top queries (2 minutes). Sort Queries by clicks and compare position for your top 20. Ignore the site-wide average: when you lose impressions on deep long-tail queries, the average can improve while traffic falls. If your top queries held their places, demand fell. Open the 16-month view and check whether the same dip happened last October.
  5. Technical, then algorithmic (2 minutes). If top queries lost 3 or more places, open Crawl stats and the Page indexing report. A rise in server errors or in "not indexed" pages on the day of the drop points to your site. Clean reports plus a drop that lines up with 16, 20, 24 or 27 October points to Google.

04Google algorithm update or technical issue: what not to do

Google's guide on debugging traffic drops says small changes in position "can happen at any time" and recommends "avoiding making radical changes if your page is already performing well". For a large drop, it says some changes take effect in a few days while others take several months, and that you'll likely want to wait a few weeks before you analyse the site in Search Console again. Four unconfirmed waves in twelve days add a further reason to hold: a page you rewrite on the 22nd can lose and regain ground on the 24th or 27th for reasons unrelated to your edit.

Write down the date and the numbers from the split today. If position loss lines up with a spike and your technical reports stay clean, compare the same windows again two weeks after the volatility settles. A drop that persists after that is a content and quality question. A drop that recovers on its own needs nothing from you.

FAQFrequently asked questions

Was there a Google algorithm update in October 2026?

Not a confirmed one, as of 1 November 2026. Google's last confirmed change was the September 2026 spam update, which ran from 24 September to 8 October. Rank trackers reported unconfirmed volatility on 16, 20 to 21, 24 and 27 October, and Google's Search Status Dashboard lists no incident since.

How do I tell if a ranking drop is an algorithm update or a site problem?

Check Search Console in order. Manual actions or security issues explain a drop on their own. If one folder holds most of the lost clicks, suspect a template or indexing change. If top queries lost three or more places and Crawl stats and Page indexing look clean, the evidence points to an algorithmic cause.

Why did my clicks drop when impressions stayed the same?

Clicks equal impressions times CTR, so steady impressions mean your click-through rate fell. Google still shows your pages, but fewer people choose them, often because an AI Overview, video block or rich result now sits above you. Rewrite titles and snippets for the queries that lost the most clicks.

How long should I wait after a Google ranking drop before changing pages?

Google says some changes take effect in a few days and others take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before analysing the site in Search Console again. Log the date and numbers now, then compare the same windows two weeks after the volatility settles.
