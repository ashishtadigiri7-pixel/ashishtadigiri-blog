Bulenox cut its 100K drawdown by $1,000. Re-size before your next trade.

Two futures prop firms tightened their numbers this week. Here is the arithmetic for re-sizing, and the rule that stops Lucid's new 40% consistency cap from costing you a payout.

In October 2026, Bulenox lowered the drawdown on its Momentum accounts, and Lucid moved its LucidFlex consistency limit from 50% to 40%. Neither change touches your strategy. Both change how many contracts you can carry and how big your best day is allowed to be. On a Bulenox 100K, last week's size now runs on a quarter less room.

What to do this week

  1. Recalculate size from the distance to your floor: room ÷ (stop × point value × losses to survive).
  2. On LucidFlex, set a daily stop-win at 40% of your profit target ($1,200 on a 50K).
  3. Check your best-day share before paying for the 50% add-on. Under 40%, it buys you nothing.

02Prop firm position sizing: size from the floor, not the balance

Room is the distance between your balance and the level where the account fails. On day one it equals the full drawdown. Losses to survive is how many full stop-outs in a row the account must absorb before it fails.

max contracts = room ÷ (stop in points × point value × losses to survive)

Worked example: MNQ at $2 per point, a 20-point stop, and enough room to survive 5 losses in a row. Each contract needs 20 × $2 × 5 = $200 of room.

Bulenox still lets a 100K trade 120 micros. At a 20-point stop, the new drawdown caps you at 15, long before the firm's contract limit comes into play.

Re-run the number every time the room changes. Halfway to the floor on the new 100K ($1,500 of room), the same setup supports 7 contracts instead of 15. Most accounts that fail near breakeven are still trading day-one size.

03Lucid's 40% consistency rule sets your daily stop-win

Lucid's consistency rule caps your best day as a share of total profit. At 40%, one big day raises the amount you need before you can pass. Turn the rule around and it gives you the number you need:

profit needed = best day ÷ 0.40
daily stop-win = 0.40 × profit target

Worked example on a LucidFlex 50K, which has a $3,000 target:

  • Best day $1,200: you need $1,200 ÷ 0.40 = $3,000, which matches the target.
  • Best day $1,800: you need $4,500 at 40%, against $3,600 under the old 50% rule. One good day adds $1,500 of profit, and the risk that comes with it, before you qualify.

So the rule becomes a daily stop-win: $1,200 on the 50K, $2,400 on the 100K ($6,000 target), $3,600 on the 150K ($9,000 target). Once you are up that much on the day, stop. Profit above it raises the total you need and gets you no closer to passing.

The same arithmetic explains Lucid's marketing line. At 40%, no single day can exceed 40% of the total, so you need at least 1 ÷ 0.40 = 2.5, rounded up to 3 winning days. At 50% it is 2. That is the "as few as 3 days" on their page.

04Is Lucid's 50% consistency add-on worth paying for?

Pull your last 20 trading days and work out your best-day share: your largest single day divided by net profit over the period. Then:

Best-day shareDecision
Under 40%Skip the add-on. It buys you nothing.
40% to 50%Price it against the extra days you would otherwise need to trade.
Over 50%Neither cap fits how you make money. A daily stop-win is the fix, not the add-on.

Lucid's evaluation page states the 40% and 50% levels but not the exact calculation method. Reports describe it as best day against total profit. Confirm in your own dashboard before relying on it.

FAQFrequently asked questions

What is the new Bulenox Momentum drawdown?

Bulenox lowered Momentum drawdowns in October 2026: the 50K went from $2,250 to $2,000, the 100K from $4,000 to $3,000 and the 150K from $5,500 to $4,500. The cut applies to both the trailing and end-of-day options. Daily loss limits and contract caps did not change.

How do I calculate position size on a prop firm account?

Divide your room to the failure level by the loss one contract takes across the losing streak you want to survive: room ÷ (stop in points × point value × losses). With $3,000 of room, a 20-point MNQ stop ($2 per point) and 5 losses, that is $3,000 ÷ $200 = 15 contracts.

What is Lucid's 40% consistency rule?

On LucidFlex evaluations your best single day can be at most 40% of your total profit, down from 50%; the 50% level is now a paid add-on. It means you need at least three winning days, and on a 50K with a $3,000 target any day above $1,200 raises the profit you need to pass.

Is Lucid's 50% consistency add-on worth paying for?

Work out your best-day share: your largest day divided by net profit over your last 20 trading days. Under 40%, the add-on buys nothing. Between 40% and 50%, price it against the extra days you would trade. Over 50%, a daily stop-win fixes the problem better than the add-on.
